Does my car insurance cover a rental car?

In most cases, yes. The coverages on your personal auto policy typically extend to a car you rent for personal use in the U.S.

Your coverage usually comes with you,
when you rent a car.

If you have liability coverage, it typically applies when you drive a rental car. If your policy includes collision and comprehensive coverage, damage to the rental car is usually covered the same way it would be for your own car, minus your deductible.

Rental companies may still charge for things your policy might not cover, such as loss of use while their car is being repaired or administrative fees. Review your policy or talk with an agent before you decline the rental company's coverage at the counter.

Before you pick up the keys

  • Confirm your policy includes collision and comprehensive
  • Know your deductible amount
  • Ask the rental company which fees they charge after damage
  • Keep your ID card handy in the Liberty Mutual app

What most states require

Nearly every state requires liability coverage. Some states require additional coverages too.

Bodily injury liability

Helps pay for injuries to other people when you cause an accident.

Property damage liability

Helps pay for damage you cause to someone else's car or property.

Additional state coverages

Some states also require personal injury protection, uninsured motorist coverage, or both.

Is the state minimum enough?
It's a starting point.

State minimums set the least coverage you can legally carry. If you cause an accident, costs above your limits could be your responsibility, so many drivers choose higher limits and add coverages like collision and comprehensive.

Is water damage from a burst pipe covered?

Typically, yes. Homeowners insurance usually covers water damage that's sudden and accidental, like a pipe that bursts. Damage from slow leaks or flooding usually isn't covered.

Sudden damage is usually covered,
gradual damage usually isn't.

If a pipe bursts and soaks your floors, walls and belongings, your dwelling and personal property coverages typically help pay for repairs and replacement, minus your deductible.

Damage that builds up over time, such as a slow drip under a sink, is usually considered a maintenance issue. Flooding from outside water, like heavy rain or an overflowing river, requires a separate flood insurance policy.

If a pipe just burst

  1. 1Turn off your main water valve
  2. 2Take photos and video of the damage
  3. 3Move belongings out of harm's way if it's safe
