Pain re-confirmation

Let them rank the pain

Goal: have them rank the pains so the demo only shows what they’ve confirmed. Sandler rule: no fulfillment without confirmed pain.

Opener · dummy curve (stay curious, don’t assume)

“From the doc you shared, we pulled out five areas: seasonal tax updates, find-and-replace, categorizing and hub linking, test planning, and personalization. If you could only fix two before next tax season, which two?”

Get these numbers

Pages touched per season · hours per update cycle · number of reviewers · error/compliance risk

Run the segments in their ranked order. Drop any segment they don’t rank.

Pain funnel · used on their #1 pick

  1. “Walk me through the last time that happened. What triggered it?”
  2. “Who does the work: Sharon, editors, or both? How long does it take?”
  3. “What have you tried? Where does Copilot help, and where does it fall short?”
  4. “What happens if an outdated 2025 figure is live in January?”
  5. “What has that cost you, in hours, risk, or delayed optimizations?”
  6. “How does the team feel about doing it again next season?”

A · Seasonal tax refresh with human approval

~10 MIN · ANCHOR PAIN

Their words: “Flag and suggest changes so Sharon can do a final check instead of researching every edit.”

On screen

  1. Give Mark JH’s 2026 figures as the source of truth (for example, a short IRS-changes brief that JH supplies).
  2. Ask Mark to search CMS content (through Graph) for articles that reference 2025 thresholds, deadlines and form names. Show the list: page, the outdated text, and the proposed change.
  3. Mark creates drafts in CMS 13. It doesn’t publish. Show the draft going into an approval sequence for Sharon.
  4. Bonus, for the “Optimizations” bucket: run Mark’s SEO analysis and GEO/AI-discoverability audit on one article and apply the recommendations.

Say it plainly

Mark works within each user’s CMS permissions. It drafts from the figures JH gives it, so CPA judgment stays with Sharon. This is the human approval step they said they need.

After the temperature check: “If your team didn’t have to start from a blank page each time, how would those hours be spent instead?”

B · CMS hygiene at scale

~7 MIN

Their words: find-and-replace, ®/™ marks, replacing images and blocks, 404 cleanup, seasonal archiving.

On screen

  1. “Find every article that mentions [old stat/phrase] and propose the replacement.” Show the list, then property updates saved as drafts.
  2. “Add ® to [brand term] on first use.” Same pattern.
  3. Upload a crawl export (CSV of 404s). Mark finds the content that links to those URLs and drafts fixes.
  4. (Tell only) Scheduling this to run on its own every season = the autonomous workflow path (phase 2, beyond Enhanced).

C · Categorize, link hubs, possibly replace Content Recs

~7 MIN

Their words: “We use Copilot outside the CMS, then update the CMS by hand.”

On screen

  1. Mark reads a new article, suggests a category from JH’s existing taxonomy, and sets the category property right in CMS 13.
  2. Mark adds the link to the matching hub page as a draft.
  3. Show (or mock) the “Related articles” block: 3 articles from the same category, pulled through a Graph query.

Say it plainly

The related-articles block is a small CMS 13 build (a block that queries Graph by category). Mark keeps the categories accurate, and that’s what makes the block work. This is the realistic route to dropping Content Recs.

Negative reverse · Copilot comparison

“Honestly, if your team is happy copying from Copilot and pasting into the CMS, that might be good enough for now. What’s the downside of keeping it that way?”

D · Experimentation that knows your data

~6 MIN

Their words: “Copilot suggests metrics we don’t even track.” “CMS content and test variations are built separately.”

On screen

  1. Mark pulls JH’s real experiments and metrics, then drafts a test plan for a high-traffic page (Refund Advance or online filing) using metrics JH actually tracks.
  2. Mark writes 2–3 variations as CMS 13 Content Variations of that page. The test content and the CMS content are now the same item.
  3. Show how the variation flows to an A/B test, and point out that Mark can pick the winning variant automatically.

Check before the call

CMS 13 content-variation A/B testing is documented with Feature Experimentation. Confirm what JH licenses today (the October notes say Web Experimentation) and adjust the story to match.

E · How personalization fits in CMS 13

~5 MIN · MOSTLY WHITEBOARD

Draw one simple map. Avoid a feature tour.

PieceWhat it’s forWhere Mark helps
Audiences (CMS 13’s name for visitor groups)Rule-based targeting of blocks (their 3–4 cross-promo groups)Drafts one cross-promo block per audience
Content VariationsMultiple published versions of one item for A/B tests, personalization, seasonal contentWrites the variations; can pick the winner
Content RecommendationsBehavior-based related content (today)Possibly replaced by category + Graph “related” block (Segment C)
Limitless 1:1 PersonalizationMark generates and updates a landing page per segment (from a CDP or similar). Real crawlable pages, not real-time per-visitor swapsCandidates: Retail/Walmart walk-in, DIY online, Refund Advance seekers

Be candid

Limitless creates pages per segment and runs alongside the site. It doesn’t do real-time per-visitor personalization.
