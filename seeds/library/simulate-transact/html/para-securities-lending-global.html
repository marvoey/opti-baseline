Setting Up a Global Securities Lending Program

Asset managers seeking to generate incremental revenue through securities lending can access CIBC Mellon's cross-border lending program, which spans Canadian, US, and European markets. Program setup requires a series of structured steps covering legal agreements, eligible securities and collateral schedules, and operational configuration across markets.

Prerequisites

  • Securities lending authority confirmed in the fund's trust deed, investment management agreement, or board resolution
  • Regulatory review completed confirming lending is permissible under applicable fund regulations (NI 81-102, UCITS, etc.)
  • Risk tolerance and indemnification requirements documented by the investment team
  • Custodial relationship with CIBC Mellon (or tri-party custodian) in place for settlement
  • Tax analysis completed for cross-border lending transactions (dividend equivalent withholding, manufactured dividends)

Process Steps

  1. Select your program structure: Choose between agent lending (CIBC Mellon acts as agent, arranging loans on your behalf) or principal lending (CIBC Mellon acts as borrower). Agent programs offer greater flexibility; principal programs provide simpler accounting treatment.
  2. Execute the Securities Lending Authorization Agreement, governing the terms under which CIBC Mellon may lend securities from your portfolio. This includes recall rights, mandatory income pass-through, and maximum on-loan thresholds.
  3. Negotiate and finalize the Eligible Securities Schedule, specifying which asset classes, securities, and markets are approved for lending (equities, fixed income, ETFs) and any exclusions (restricted securities, ESG screens).
  4. Negotiate and finalize the Collateral Schedule, covering accepted collateral types (cash, government securities, letters of credit), collateral haircuts by asset type, and concentration limits.
  5. Execute the Indemnification Agreement, under which CIBC Mellon indemnifies you against borrower default in agent lending programs, subject to collateral coverage thresholds.
  6. Configure global market access for each target market: Canada (through CDS), United States (through DTC/NSCC), and Europe (through Euroclear or Clearstream). Each market may require separate bilateral agreements with approved borrowers.
  7. Confirm reporting and transparency requirements: Agree on daily loan activity reporting, collateral mark-to-market frequency, revenue attribution, and regulatory reporting under SFTR (for European positions) or SEC Rule 10c-1a (for US positions).
  8. Complete operational testing with CIBC Mellon's Securities Finance team and launch the program on the agreed effective date.

Program Configuration and Launch Steps

Indemnification and Risk Framework

CIBC Mellon's agent lending indemnification covers borrower default exposure up to the value of collateral held at the time of default. Clients retain investment risk on cash collateral reinvestment. Discuss your risk tolerance with the Securities Finance team to determine the appropriate collateral reinvestment guidelines and cash collateral fund options.

Global Market Access Setup

  • Canada: Lending through CDSX; CDS participant status required or managed via CIBC Mellon's nominee
  • United States: DTC delivery-versus-payment lending; NSCC membership or agent arrangement required
  • Europe: Cross-border lending via Euroclear Bank or Clearstream Banking Luxembourg; subject to SFTR reporting obligations
  • Global borrower network: CIBC Mellon maintains approved borrower lists across all markets; new borrower approvals require credit committee review

Program Launch Timeline

  • Legal agreement negotiation and execution: 4–8 weeks
  • Schedule finalization and market setup: 2–4 weeks (concurrent with agreement review)
  • Operational testing: 1–2 weeks
  • Total estimated timeline: 8–14 weeks from initial engagement

What Happens Next

Once live, your program activity is visible in the CIBC Mellon client portal, with daily loan and collateral reports. Revenue is accrued and reported monthly, with settlement of net lending revenue according to your agreed disbursement schedule.
