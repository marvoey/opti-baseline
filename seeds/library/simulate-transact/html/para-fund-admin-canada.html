Pension Fund Administration Transition Process

Transitioning a domestic Canadian pension fund's administration to CIBC Mellon requires careful sequencing across regulatory notification, data migration, and operational cutover. This process is designed to minimize disruption to plan members while ensuring full continuity of benefit payments and regulatory filings throughout the transition period.

Prerequisites

  • Current administrator's termination notice period confirmed and contractually managed
  • Pension plan text, investment policy statement, and most recent actuarial valuation available for transfer
  • Plan sponsor board or pension committee resolution authorizing the transition
  • PBSA (Pension Benefits Standards Act) or applicable provincial pension legislation compliance review completed
  • Legal counsel engaged to review transition agreement, indemnification terms, and data ownership provisions

Process Steps

  1. Initiate the RFP or sole-source engagement with CIBC Mellon Relationship Management. Provide current AUM, member count, benefit structure (DB, DC, or hybrid), and transition target date.
  2. Execute the Transition Services Agreement (TSA), documenting responsibilities of incumbent administrator, CIBC Mellon, and plan sponsor during the transition period.
  3. Deliver the discovery data package to CIBC Mellon's Transition Management team, including member census files, contribution history, investment records, benefit payment schedules, and outstanding claim files.
  4. Complete regulatory notification to OSFI (for federally regulated plans) or the applicable provincial regulator (e.g., FSRA in Ontario, RREQ in Quebec) as required under applicable pension legislation.
  5. Enter the parallel run phase, during which CIBC Mellon processes all administration transactions in parallel with the incumbent. Discrepancies are logged and resolved before cutover approval.
  6. Receive sign-off from plan sponsor and auditors on parallel run results, confirming data accuracy and benefit calculation parity.
  7. Execute the cutover on the agreed transition date. CIBC Mellon assumes full operational responsibility from this date, including member inquiries, benefit payments, and regulatory filings.
  8. Complete post-cutover reconciliation within 30 days to confirm all outstanding items from the parallel run period are resolved.

Data Migration and Cutover Steps

Data Migration Checklist

  • Member census: active members, deferred vested, retirees, and beneficiaries
  • Contribution history: employee and employer contributions by member and tax year
  • Investment account balances and unit holdings (for DC components)
  • Defined benefit service records and pensionable earnings history
  • Benefit payment instructions (EFT details, tax withholding elections, currency)
  • Outstanding benefit calculations, pending appeals, and open files
  • RRSP/DPSP transfer authorizations and CRA reporting history

Regulatory Filing Continuity

CIBC Mellon coordinates with the plan sponsor's legal counsel and actuary to ensure no regulatory filing deadline is missed during the transition window. This includes annual information returns under PBSA or applicable provincial legislation, T4A slip preparation and CRA remittance continuity, and any interim pension committee reporting obligations.

Service Level Agreement Setup

The SLA, finalized during the TSA negotiation phase, covers benefit payment processing timelines, member inquiry response standards, statement issuance frequency, and audit support commitments. SLA performance reporting begins on the first full calendar month following cutover.

Expected Timeline

  • Discovery and agreement execution: 4–8 weeks
  • Parallel run: 2–4 months (depending on plan complexity)
  • Cutover and post-reconciliation: 4–6 weeks
  • Total transition window: 6–12 months from RFP engagement
