Treasury Portal Enrollment Process

Corporate treasury teams can access CIBC Mellon's institutional cash management portal for Canadian operations by completing a structured enrollment and configuration process. This workflow covers entity verification, account structure selection, authorized user provisioning, and technical integration — from initial setup through go-live testing.

Prerequisites

  • Corporate entity registered in Canada or maintaining a Canadian branch with an active banking relationship
  • Board or treasury committee resolution authorizing CIBC Mellon as cash management provider
  • Authorized signatories identified and specimen signatures provided
  • Internal IT or treasury systems team available for API or file feed configuration
  • Existing account structure documentation (legal entities, subsidiaries, CAD and USD accounts) provided

Process Steps

  1. Submit the Treasury Services Enrollment Form to your CIBC Mellon Relationship Manager, including entity list, account structure diagram, and preferred cash management objectives.
  2. Complete entity verification with CIBC Mellon's KYC/Onboarding team. Provide corporate registration certificates, authorized signatory lists, and LEI confirmation for each entity in scope.
  3. Select your account structure: Notional pooling consolidates balances across accounts for interest calculation without physical movement of funds; zero-balance sweeping physically concentrates cash into a master account on an intraday or end-of-day basis. Your CIBC Mellon Treasury Advisor will model both structures based on your cash flow patterns.
  4. Execute the Cash Management Master Agreement and applicable account-specific schedules. Each legal entity must execute its own authorization where required by corporate governance rules.
  5. Set up authorized users in the CIBC Mellon treasury portal. Define user roles (view-only, payment initiation, payment authorization, administration), assign approval hierarchies, and configure dual-control requirements for payment instructions above defined thresholds.
  6. Configure your API connection or SWIFT/file feed depending on your preferred integration method (see technical setup section below).
  7. Complete user acceptance testing (UAT) in the CIBC Mellon sandbox environment, covering payment initiation, balance reporting, file import/export, and notification workflows.
  8. Proceed to go-live after UAT sign-off. CIBC Mellon's Implementation team provides hypercare support for the first 30 days post-launch.

Account Structure and System Configuration

Notional Pooling vs. Zero-Balance Sweeping

Notional pooling is well-suited for multi-entity corporate groups where legal or regulatory restrictions prevent intercompany fund transfers. Interest is calculated on the net pool position, with individual account balances remaining legally distinct. Zero-balance sweeping is preferred where treasury centralization and simplified intercompany accounting are priorities; it requires transfer pricing documentation for tax purposes in Canada.

API and File Feed Configuration

  • REST API: OAuth 2.0 authentication; supports real-time balance inquiries, payment initiation (CAD, USD, wire, EFT), and transaction history retrieval
  • SWIFT connectivity: MT101, MT940/942, and camt.053 ISO 20022 message support; confirm your SWIFT BIC and service bureau arrangement
  • File-based integration: CSV and BAI2 format for balance reporting; PAIN.001 for payment instruction files
  • IP allowlisting and TLS certificate setup: Required for API connections; CIBC Mellon provides technical specifications and sandbox credentials

Go-Live Checklist

  • UAT sign-off document completed and countersigned by CIBC Mellon Implementation Manager
  • All authorized users have completed portal training and 2FA enrollment
  • Payment thresholds and dual-control rules confirmed in production environment
  • First production balance report received and reconciled to internal records
  • Hypercare support contact details provided to treasury team
