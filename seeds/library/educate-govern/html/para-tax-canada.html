Canadian Withholding Tax for Foreign Institutions

Foreign institutional investors holding Canadian securities are subject to withholding tax on certain passive income payments under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada) (ITA). Part XIII imposes a 25% statutory withholding rate on dividends, interest (other than arm's-length interest exempt under subsection 212(1)(b)), management fees, rents, royalties, and certain other amounts paid or credited to non-residents of Canada. The obligation to withhold and remit falls on the Canadian payer — typically the issuer, trustee, or custodian — and failure to do so results in joint and several liability between the payer and the non-resident.

Interest Income

Arm's-length interest paid by a Canadian resident to a non-resident is generally exempt from Part XIII withholding under the ITA, provided the debt obligation was issued after June 23, 1975, and the debt is not a participating debt interest. Foreign institutions receiving interest from Canadian corporate bonds, bank deposits, or money market instruments in an arm's-length context should confirm that the obligation meets the statutory exemption criteria before assuming zero withholding applies.

Dividends

Dividends paid by Canadian corporations to non-resident shareholders are subject to 25% Part XIII withholding unless a tax treaty provides for a reduced rate. The statutory rate applies in the absence of treaty relief, and sub-custodians must apply the correct treaty rate based on verified residency documentation before making payment.

Treaty Relief and Reporting Obligations

US–Canada Tax Treaty

Under Article X of the Canada–United States Income Tax Convention (1980, as amended), the withholding rate on dividends is reduced to:

  • 5% for dividends paid to a US company holding at least 10% of the voting stock of the Canadian payer
  • 15% for all other US resident beneficial owners

The treaty also eliminates withholding on arm's-length interest and royalties in most cases. Treaty benefits are available only to beneficial owners who are residents of the US, and portfolio investors (e.g., US pension funds, mutual funds) qualifying as treaty residents must provide adequate documentation to claim reduced rates.

UK–Canada Tax Treaty

Under the Canada–United Kingdom Tax Convention (1978), the withholding rate on dividends is generally reduced to 15%, with a 5% rate available where a UK company beneficially owns at least 10% of the capital of the Canadian paying corporation. Post-Brexit, this treaty continues to govern Canada–UK withholding treatment independently of Canada's treaties with EU member states.

NR4 Reporting Obligations

Canadian payers must file an NR4 information return (Form NR4) with the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) for each calendar year by March 31 of the following year. The NR4 reports amounts paid or credited to non-residents, the withholding tax deducted, and the country of residence of the payee. Non-resident investors should ensure their custodian or sub-custodian holds accurate residency and treaty entitlement documentation to support correct NR4 coding.

Stock Dividends and ETF Distributions

Stock dividends paid by Canadian corporations to non-residents are treated as taxable dividends under Part XIII and are subject to the same withholding regime as cash dividends. ETF distributions from Canadian-listed ETFs may comprise a mix of income types — dividends, capital gains, return of capital, and interest — each attracting different withholding treatment. Custodians must disaggregate distribution components and apply the appropriate withholding rate to each component.

FATCA and CRS Reciprocal Reporting

Canada has implemented the Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act (FATCA) through an Intergovernmental Agreement (IGA) with the United States and the Common Reporting Standard (CRS) through domestic legislation (Part XIX of the ITA). Canadian financial institutions, including sub-custodians, are required to:

  • Identify accounts held by US persons (FATCA) or non-resident persons (CRS) through prescribed due diligence procedures
  • Report specified account information to the CRA, which exchanges data with treaty partners under FATCA (US) and CRS (over 100 participating jurisdictions)
  • Collect self-certification forms (e.g., CRS Self-Certification, IRS Form W-8 series) from account holders and retain these for a minimum of six years
