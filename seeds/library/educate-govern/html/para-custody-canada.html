Withholding Tax Entitlements for Foreign Custody Clients

Foreign institutional investors holding Canadian securities through a Canadian sub-custodian — including arrangements where CIBC Mellon acts as sub-custodian to global custodians and prime brokers — are entitled to treaty-reduced withholding rates on Canadian-source dividends and interest, provided they qualify as beneficial owners resident in a jurisdiction with which Canada has a tax treaty and meet the applicable treaty eligibility conditions. Failing to claim and document treaty entitlement correctly results in withholding at the statutory 25% rate under Part XIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada), creating the potential for overpayment, refund claim complexity, and reconciliation burdens for the sub-custodian's nominee chain.

Treaty-Reduced Withholding Rates

Canada has concluded tax treaties with over 90 countries. The most frequently accessed treaty rates for institutional investors are:

  • United States: 15% on portfolio dividends; 5% for corporate holders with 10%+ voting share ownership; 0% on arm's-length interest and royalties
  • United Kingdom: 15% on portfolio dividends; 5% for corporate holders with 10%+ capital interest; 0% on most arm's-length interest
  • European Union member states: Rates vary by bilateral treaty — typically 15% on dividends and 0% on arm's-length interest for major treaty partners (Germany, France, Netherlands, Luxembourg)
  • Japan: 15% on portfolio dividends; 5% for corporate holders with 25%+ ownership; 0% on arm's-length interest

Treaty rates apply only to beneficial owners — nominees, custodians, and clearing intermediaries holding on behalf of clients cannot claim treaty rates on their own account. The sub-custodian must look through the nominee chain to establish the treaty entitlement of the underlying beneficial owner.

CDS Eligibility for Treaty Clients

Canadian securities are settled and held through CDS Clearing and Depository Services Inc. (CDS), Canada's central securities depository. CDS operates a Direct Registration System (DRS) and a nominee holder model, whereby most institutional holdings are maintained in the name of CDS's nominee, CDS & Co. The CDS Foreign Investor Program allows foreign institutional investors, through their Canadian sub-custodians, to hold securities in a manner that supports treaty entitlement claims. Sub-custodians must ensure that CDS account structures correctly segregate holdings by beneficial owner country to facilitate proper withholding tax treatment at the issuer and transfer agent level.

Treaty Certification and Compliance

NR301, NR302, and NR303 Forms

The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) requires that treaty entitlement claims be supported by appropriate certification forms:

  • NR301 — Declaration of Eligibility for Benefits Under a Tax Treaty for a Non-Resident Person: Used by individual non-resident beneficial owners claiming treaty benefits; certifies country of residence, treaty eligibility, and beneficial ownership status
  • NR302 — Declaration of Eligibility for Benefits Under a Tax Treaty for a Partnership With Non-Resident Partners: Used where the beneficial owner is a partnership with non-resident partners; requires disclosure of each partner's residency and pro-rata entitlement
  • NR303 — Declaration of Eligibility for Benefits Under a Tax Treaty for a Hybrid Entity: Used for entities that are fiscally transparent in one jurisdiction (e.g., a US LLC treated as a pass-through for US tax purposes) but are residents of a treaty country for Canadian tax purposes

Sub-custodians must collect valid NR-series certifications from their clients (or from the global custodian on behalf of the underlying beneficial owner) prior to applying reduced treaty rates. Forms are generally valid for three years but must be updated upon any change in the beneficial owner's residency status. Where certifications are not received, the sub-custodian must withhold at the statutory 25% rate and remit to the CRA.

FATCA Withholding Agent Obligations

As a Canadian financial institution, a Canadian sub-custodian acting as a withholding agent for payments to non-US persons must comply with Canada's FATCA Intergovernmental Agreement (IGA). Under the Model 1 IGA signed between Canada and the United States, Canadian financial institutions report US reportable accounts directly to the CRA (rather than to the IRS), and the CRA exchanges this information with the IRS under the existing tax information exchange framework. Sub-custodians must:

  • Conduct FATCA due diligence on all account holders to identify US persons and US-owned foreign entity accounts
  • Collect IRS Form W-8BEN, W-8BEN-E, W-8ECI, or W-8EXP as applicable from non-US account holders to establish foreign status and treaty eligibility
  • Report US reportable accounts to the CRA annually under Part XVIII of the Income Tax Act (Canada)

Qualified Intermediary Agreement Implications

Foreign financial institutions (FFIs) that are party to a Qualified Intermediary (QI) agreement with the IRS — a separate regime from FATCA that applies to payments of US-source income — must similarly provide treaty certification documentation when receiving Canadian dividends on US persons' behalf. While the QI agreement specifically governs US withholding tax, its documentation standards (IRS W-8 and W-9 forms) are frequently cross-referenced by Canadian sub-custodians as supporting evidence of beneficial ownership and treaty residence for Canadian withholding tax purposes. Sub-custodians should confirm with their legal and tax advisors that documentation collected under QI or FATCA regimes satisfies the separate evidentiary standards applicable under Canadian Part XIII withholding rules.
