ESG Climate Risk Disclosure Framework

Canadian corporate pension plan sponsors face a converging set of ESG and climate risk disclosure obligations driven by both federal prudential regulation and securities law. The primary regulatory anchor is OSFI Guideline B-15, Climate Risk Management, which came into effect January 1, 2024, and applies to all federally regulated financial institutions (FRFIs) — including federally regulated pension plan administrators and insurance holding companies. Simultaneously, the Canadian Securities Administrators (CSA) have issued guidance through Staff Notice 51-358 addressing climate-related disclosure obligations for reporting issuers, and the Canadian Association of Pension Supervisory Authorities (CAPSA) has published updated guidelines on ESG integration at the plan level.

Canada's climate disclosure framework draws heavily from the recommendations of the Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures (TCFD), which organized disclosure across four pillars: Governance, Strategy, Risk Management, and Metrics & Targets. The International Sustainability Standards Board (ISSB), which absorbed TCFD in 2023, has published IFRS S2 Climate-related Disclosures, and Canadian standard-setters are actively aligning domestic requirements with IFRS S2. Corporate pension sponsors should assume that ISSB-aligned disclosure obligations will form the baseline for Canadian mandatory requirements in the near term.

OSFI Guideline B-15: Climate Risk Management

Guideline B-15 requires FRFIs to embed climate risk management into their existing governance and risk frameworks. Key obligations applicable to pension administrators and insurance holding companies include:

  • Board and senior management accountability for climate risk oversight, with clear articulation of climate risk appetite
  • Integration of climate-related physical and transition risks into enterprise risk management frameworks
  • Climate scenario analysis encompassing at least two scenarios, including a 1.5°C or well-below-2°C scenario aligned with the Paris Agreement
  • Disclosure of climate-related financial risks and opportunities in annual reporting, following TCFD/ISSB structure
  • Development of internal targets and metrics for measuring and monitoring climate risk exposures over time

CSA Staff Notice 51-358: Climate-Related Disclosures

CSA Staff Notice 51-358, issued in August 2019 and supplemented by subsequent consultation, clarifies that existing continuous disclosure obligations under securities law require reporting issuers to disclose material climate-related risks in their annual information forms (AIFs) and MD&A. The CSA has since launched formal rulemaking for mandatory TCFD-aligned disclosure. Corporate plan sponsors that are also reporting issuers (e.g., publicly-listed plan sponsors) must ensure alignment between securities law climate disclosures and any climate-related communications directed at plan beneficiaries.

Implementation Timeline and Milestones

The regulatory timeline for Canadian climate risk disclosure is phased to allow institutions to build capability progressively:

  • January 1, 2024: OSFI Guideline B-15 effective for all FRFIs — baseline governance and risk management expectations apply immediately
  • Fiscal Year 2024 Reporting: First mandatory TCFD-aligned qualitative disclosures for large FRFIs (total assets exceeding CAD 10 billion) in annual reports and public filings
  • Fiscal Year 2025 Reporting: Quantitative Scope 1 and Scope 2 greenhouse gas (GHG) emission disclosures required for large FRFIs; climate scenario analysis results must be disclosed
  • Fiscal Year 2026 Reporting: Scope 3 GHG emission disclosures and financed emissions disclosure expected for large FRFIs; smaller FRFIs subject to phased-in requirements
  • CSA Mandatory Rule (anticipated 2026): Mandatory ISSB S2-aligned disclosure for all reporting issuers, pending final rulemaking; draft rule published for comment in early 2025

CAPSA Guidelines on ESG for Pension Plans

CAPSA Guideline No. 8, Defined Benefit Pension Plan Funding Policy, and the broader CAPSA ESG guidance note (published 2022) set out expectations for pension plan administrators on integrating ESG factors into investment policies and risk management. Key expectations include:

  • The Statement of Investment Policies and Procedures (SIPP) must address whether and how ESG factors, including climate risk, are incorporated into investment decision-making
  • Administrators are not required to adopt specific ESG strategies, but must engage in a deliberate process and document their conclusions
  • Regular review of ESG-related provisions in the SIPP, at minimum annually, or following material changes in the regulatory environment
  • Reporting to plan members and beneficiaries on ESG integration approaches where material to the plan's investment strategy
