UCITS V: Key Directive Requirements

Directive 2014/91/EU (UCITS V) came into force on March 18, 2016, amending the original UCITS IV framework to strengthen investor protection, align depositary standards with those introduced under AIFMD, and introduce mandatory remuneration disclosure for fund managers. UCITS V significantly raised the governance bar for depositaries and management companies operating in the EU, with implications for fund administrators who support downstream compliance and reporting obligations. All EU member states were required to transpose UCITS V into national law by March 18, 2016, though transposition timelines and specific national implementation rules vary across jurisdictions.

Depositary Liability Under UCITS V

UCITS V introduced a strict liability standard for depositaries with respect to the loss of financial instruments held in custody. Under Article 24 of the Directive, a depositary is strictly liable for any loss of financial instruments held in custody — including by sub-custodians — unless it can demonstrate the loss arose from an external event beyond its reasonable control and whose consequences were unavoidable. This is a materially higher standard than previously applied and cannot be discharged by contract. For fund administrators, this creates an obligation to ensure that reconciliation processes, custody confirmations, and sub-custodian oversight frameworks are robust enough to detect and escalate losses in a timely manner.

Remuneration Policy Disclosure

UCITS V mandates that management companies disclose details of their remuneration policy, including the total amount of remuneration paid to all staff and the aggregate remuneration paid to staff whose actions have a material impact on the risk profile of the UCITS. The disclosure must appear in the fund's annual report and on the management company's website. Fund administrators supporting UCITS management companies must accommodate these disclosure requirements in their financial statement production and annual reporting workflows.

Eligible Assets and Investment Restrictions

UCITS funds remain subject to strict investment restrictions under the UCITS framework and ESMA's eligible assets guidelines (CESR/07-044b). Permissible investments are limited to:

  • Transferable securities and money market instruments admitted to or dealt on a regulated market
  • Units of other UCITS or eligible UCIs, subject to concentration limits
  • Deposits with credit institutions meeting prescribed criteria
  • Derivatives on eligible underlying assets, subject to risk management procedures approved by the competent authority

Fund administrators are expected to monitor portfolio compliance with these restrictions on each dealing day, flagging breaches to portfolio managers and compliance functions in accordance with the fund's breach escalation policy.

Depositary and Operational Impacts

KID/PRIIP Documentation Requirements

Effective January 1, 2023, UCITS funds sold to retail investors are no longer exempt from the PRIIPs Regulation (EU) No 1286/2014, and must produce a Key Information Document (KID) under the PRIIPs framework in lieu of the UCITS Key Investor Information Document (KIID). The PRIIPs KID imposes standardised performance scenario calculations, Summary Risk Indicator (SRI) methodology, and cost disclosure templates. Fund administrators must integrate PRIIPs-compliant calculation engines into their fund accounting and document management platforms to ensure accurate and timely KID production across all share classes.

ESMA Liquidity Stress Testing Guidelines

ESMA's Guidelines on Liquidity Stress Testing in UCITS and AIFs (ESMA/2019/ESMA34-39-897), which became applicable from September 30, 2020, require UCITS management companies to implement documented liquidity stress testing (LST) programmes proportionate to the nature, scale, and complexity of the fund. Required components include:

  • Asset-side stress testing covering market impact, redemption shocks, and correlated liquidity deterioration scenarios
  • Liability-side stress testing modelling investor redemption behaviour under stressed market conditions
  • Minimum frequency of at least annual for all UCITS, with more frequent testing for funds with heightened liquidity risk
  • Board-level review and sign-off on LST methodology, assumptions, and results
  • Documented escalation procedures where LST results indicate liquidity concerns

Fund administrators with responsibility for fund accounting, NAV calculation, and portfolio data aggregation play a critical supporting role in supplying clean, timely data inputs to management company liquidity stress testing models.
