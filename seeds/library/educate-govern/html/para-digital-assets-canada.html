Regulatory Framework for Digital Assets in Canadian Funds

The Canadian regulatory framework for digital assets held within registered investment funds remains in active development, with the Canadian Securities Administrators (CSA) serving as the primary securities regulatory authority and OSFI establishing prudential expectations for federally regulated institutions with digital asset exposures. The regime has been shaped by several years of crypto asset fund prospectus reviews, targeted staff notices, and ongoing CSA rulemaking activity. Asset managers seeking to include crypto assets within Canadian mutual funds or exchange-traded funds must navigate a layered set of requirements under National Instrument 81-102 (NI 81-102), CSA guidance documents, and the evolving OSFI position on institutional crypto exposures.

CSA Staff Notice 81-336: Guidance on Crypto Asset Funds

CSA Staff Notice 81-336, published in August 2022, consolidates guidance for investment funds seeking to obtain exposure to crypto assets and sets out disclosure, risk management, and structural requirements that the CSA will apply in prospectus reviews. Key points of Staff Notice 81-336 include:

  • Crypto asset funds must hold crypto assets directly or through regulated intermediaries; indirect exposure via derivatives is subject to additional scrutiny
  • Managers must demonstrate the existence of adequate pricing, valuation, liquidity, and custody arrangements before a prospectus is receipted
  • Investment objectives, strategies, and risk disclosures must specifically address the unique risks of crypto assets, including key management risk, exchange risk, regulatory risk, and technological risk
  • Valuation policies must be pre-approved by the fund's IRC and must address situations where principal market pricing is unavailable or unreliable

Eligible Investment Criteria Under NI 81-102

NI 81-102 does not currently designate crypto assets as a defined eligible asset class for conventional mutual funds. Crypto asset funds that have received prospectus receipts from the CSA have done so under prospectus-level accommodations and subject-to conditions negotiated during the review process. Key NI 81-102 constraints relevant to crypto assets include:

  • The 10% illiquid asset restriction, which may apply where crypto assets trade on unregulated venues or lack demonstrable secondary market depth
  • Counterparty concentration limits that bear on exchange custody arrangements
  • Restrictions on investing in non-publicly traded securities that may apply to certain tokenised assets or DeFi instruments

Custodial and Eligibility Requirements

Custodian Qualification for Digital Assets

NI 81-102 and NI 81-104 (commodity pools) require that fund assets be held by a qualified custodian. The CSA has determined that crypto assets must be held by custodians that meet specific criteria adapted from the traditional custodian qualification framework. Qualified crypto custodians must:

  • Be incorporated or organised under Canadian or foreign law as a financial institution, trust company, or registered dealer
  • Hold crypto assets in segregated accounts maintained separate from the custodian's proprietary assets
  • Maintain insurance or equivalent financial arrangements covering loss of private keys, exchange hacks, and employee theft
  • Implement cold storage requirements for a material proportion of assets under custody, with documented access controls and key management procedures
  • Provide the fund with regular reconciliation and audit-ready records of all holdings

OSFI Treatment of Crypto-Asset Exposures

OSFI's Advisory on Crypto-Assets (issued November 2022 and subsequently updated) aligns with the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision's (BCBS) standard on the prudential treatment of crypto-asset exposures, published in December 2022. Under this framework:

  • Group 1 crypto assets (tokenised traditional assets and stablecoins meeting strict conditions) qualify for risk-weight treatment analogous to the underlying asset
  • Group 2 crypto assets (unbacked crypto assets, including Bitcoin and Ether) are subject to a conservative 1250% risk weight, effectively requiring dollar-for-dollar capital against the exposure
  • OSFI expects FRFIs to maintain board-approved policies governing any direct or indirect crypto exposure, including through fund investments or securities lending of crypto ETF units

Risk Disclosure Obligations

All public offering documents for crypto asset funds must include prominent disclosure of risks specific to digital assets. Regulators expect disclosure to address, at minimum: price volatility and liquidity risk; custody and key management risk; regulatory risk, including the risk of future prohibition; technological risk including smart contract vulnerabilities; and environmental risk associated with proof-of-work consensus mechanisms where applicable.
