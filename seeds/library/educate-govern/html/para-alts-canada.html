Alternative Investments: Canadian Regulatory Framework

Canadian pension funds and other institutional investors accessing alternative asset classes — including private equity, infrastructure, real estate, hedge funds, and private debt — operate within a regulatory environment shaped primarily by the federal Pension Benefits Standards Act, 1985 (PBSA) and its provincial equivalents, OSFI prudential guidance, and the Investment Policy Statement (IPS) requirements that govern federally regulated pension plans. Unlike traditional asset classes subject to prescriptive quantity limits in earlier regulatory regimes, Canadian pension regulation has adopted a "prudent person" standard that gives plan administrators wide latitude to invest in non-traditional asset classes — provided the investment decision process meets the standard of care expected of a reasonably prudent person with comparable responsibilities.

OSFI Guidance on Non-Traditional Asset Classes

OSFI has not issued a stand-alone guideline specifically governing alternative investments for pension funds, but its broader supervisory expectations are communicated through its Supervisory Framework, Guideline E-21 (Operational Risk and Resilience), and targeted letters to pension plan administrators. OSFI expects that pension administrators investing in non-traditional asset classes maintain:

  • A clearly documented rationale demonstrating how alternative allocations contribute to the plan's overall risk-return objectives
  • Adequate due diligence processes covering manager selection, fee structures, fund governance, and ongoing monitoring
  • Liquidity stress testing that accounts for the illiquid nature of many alternative assets and potential for capital call obligations
  • Concentration risk analysis preventing any single alternative manager, fund strategy, or asset category from representing a disproportionate share of plan assets

Investment Policy Statement Requirements Under the PBSA

Under section 7.1 of the PBSA, the administrator of every pension plan must establish, maintain, and periodically review a Statement of Investment Policies and Procedures (SIPP). For pension plans with significant alternative investment allocations, the SIPP must address:

  • Specific alternative investment categories permitted, including maximum allocation ranges by category (e.g., private equity up to 15% of total assets)
  • Valuation methodology for each alternative asset category, including the frequency of third-party appraisals
  • Use of leverage at the plan level and through fund-of-fund structures, with defined maximum leverage ratios
  • Co-investment and direct investment policies where the plan invests alongside private equity sponsors or infrastructure operators
  • Governance procedures for approving, monitoring, and terminating alternative investment mandates

OSFI expects SIPP reviews to occur at a minimum annually, with more frequent review triggered by material changes in market conditions, plan liability profile, or the regulatory environment. Material amendments to the SIPP must be filed with OSFI within 60 days.

Valuation, Leverage, and Reporting Standards

Valuation Standards for Illiquid Assets

Pension administrators must adopt valuation policies for alternative assets that meet the standard of care under the PBSA. Industry standard-setters — particularly the CFA Institute's Global Investment Performance Standards (GIPS) and the International Private Equity and Venture Capital Valuation (IPEV) Guidelines — provide the widely accepted framework for private equity and real asset valuation. Key requirements include:

  • Valuations of private equity and infrastructure holdings conducted at least annually by qualified independent appraisers, with more frequent valuations required where material value-affecting events occur
  • Real estate holdings appraised by members of the Appraisal Institute of Canada (AIC) or equivalent, using recognised methodologies (income capitalisation, discounted cash flow, comparable sales)
  • Use of Net Asset Value (NAV) provided by fund managers for fund-of-fund positions, with the plan administrator retaining responsibility for challenging NAVs where red flags are identified
  • Documentation of valuation decisions, including any adjustments made to manager-provided valuations

ILPA Reporting Standards

The Institutional Limited Partners Association (ILPA) Reporting Template, most recently updated in its 2016 Standardised Reporting Template version, provides a widely adopted framework for reporting between general partners (GPs) and limited partners (LPs) in private equity funds. Canadian pension plans investing in private equity funds should request ILPA-compliant quarterly and annual reports from GPs, covering: capital contributions and distributions, NAV reconciliation, management fees and carried interest calculations, portfolio company performance summaries, and ESG metrics where applicable.

Leverage Restrictions

The PBSA does not impose an explicit maximum leverage ratio on pension plan alternative investment portfolios, but OSFI's prudent person expectations require that administrators have a clear understanding and active governance of leverage used at both the plan and underlying fund level. Leverage employed within private equity or real estate fund structures counts as economic leverage even when not reflected on the plan's consolidated balance sheet, and must be factored into liquidity stress testing and risk reporting frameworks.
