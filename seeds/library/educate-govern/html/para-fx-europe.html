MiFID II Best Execution for FX Transactions

Directive 2014/65/EU (MiFID II) and its implementing regulation MiFIR introduced a comprehensive best execution regime that applies to all investment firms operating in the European Economic Area (EEA), including asset managers executing FX transactions in connection with portfolio management and order execution services. Article 27 of MiFID II requires investment firms to take all sufficient steps to obtain the best possible result for clients when executing orders, taking into account price, costs, speed, likelihood of execution and settlement, size, nature, and any other relevant consideration. For FX transactions executed in connection with fund subscriptions, redemptions, and portfolio rebalancing, best execution obligations apply to the full dealing chain, including the spot and forward FX legs.

The European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA) has clarified through its Q&A on MiFID II investor protection topics (ESMA35-43-349) that FX transactions executed in connection with securities transactions — so-called "necessary FX" — are subject to best execution obligations. Asset managers cannot rely on a blanket exemption for ancillary FX activity. This has driven significant investment in FX execution management systems (EMS), TCA (Transaction Cost Analysis) capabilities, and written FX execution policies that must be maintained, reviewed, and communicated to clients.

Article 27 Best Execution Obligations

Under Article 27 of MiFID II, investment firms must:

  • Establish and implement a written Order Execution Policy covering all financial instruments and execution venues used, including FX venues and counterparty banks
  • Obtain prior client consent to the Order Execution Policy before providing order execution services
  • Monitor the effectiveness of the execution policy and arrangements on an ongoing basis, including regular TCA review
  • Review execution arrangements at least annually, or whenever a material change occurs that could affect the firm's ability to continue to obtain the best possible result
  • Demonstrate at the request of a client how the firm's execution arrangements satisfy its best execution obligations

Pre-Trade Transparency for FX Derivatives

MiFID II / MiFIR pre-trade transparency requirements apply to FX derivatives that are admitted to trading or traded on a regulated venue (RM, MTF, or OTF). Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) and FX options listed on OTFs are subject to pre-trade quote publication requirements unless a waiver (e.g., size-specific waiver or illiquid instrument waiver) has been granted by the relevant competent authority. OTC FX spot transactions and most outright FX forwards used for commercial hedging remain outside the MiFID II pre-trade transparency perimeter.

Reporting and Transparency Obligations

RTS 28: Annual Best Execution Reporting

Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/576 (RTS 28) requires investment firms that execute orders on behalf of clients to publish, for each class of financial instruments, information on the top five execution venues used and a summary of the quality of execution obtained. For FX, the relevant asset class is "Foreign Exchange Derivatives" or "Foreign Exchange" depending on the instrument type. Required disclosures under RTS 28 include:

  • The identity of the top five execution venues ranked by trading volume for the preceding calendar year
  • A summary of the analysis and conclusions drawn from monitoring execution quality against the firm's execution policy
  • Details of any close links, conflicts of interest, or common ownership with respect to the execution venues used
  • Information on whether the firm received any payments, discounts, rebates, or non-monetary benefits from execution venues

RTS 28 reports must be published annually, no later than April 30 for the preceding calendar year, and must remain publicly available for five years. Following Brexit, UK-based investment firms produce equivalent annual best execution reports under the UK retained version of RTS 28, with the same publication deadline.

EMIR FX Derivative Reporting

Regulation (EU) 648/2012 (EMIR) requires all counterparties to OTC derivative contracts to report trade details to a registered trade repository no later than the working day following the conclusion, modification, or termination of the contract. FX forwards with a maturity of more than one business day — and all FX options and NDFs — are in scope for EMIR reporting. Under EMIR Refit (Regulation (EU) 2019/834), the reporting obligation was clarified so that financial counterparties (FCs) bear responsibility for reporting on behalf of non-financial counterparties below the clearing threshold (NFC-). Asset managers that are financial counterparties must maintain complete and accurate trade repository submissions and reconcile positions with their FX counterparty banks on an ongoing basis.

ESMA Transaction Reporting Guidelines

In addition to EMIR reporting, FX derivatives within MiFID II scope are subject to transaction reporting under Article 26 of MiFIR. Investment firms must submit complete and accurate transaction reports to their National Competent Authority (NCA) by the close of the following working day, covering all transactions in financial instruments admitted to trading on an EU trading venue, regardless of where the transaction was executed. ESMA's guidelines on transaction reporting (ESMA70-1861941480-56) provide detailed field-level guidance, including identification of the trading capacity, counterparty identification using LEIs, and instrument identification using ISINs where available.
