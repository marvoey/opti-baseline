US T+1 Settlement: Regulatory Background

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) adopted its T+1 settlement cycle rule on February 15, 2023, with an effective date of May 28, 2024. The rule amends Rule 15c6-1(a) under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, reducing the standard settlement cycle for most broker-dealer transactions in securities from two business days (T+2) to one business day (T+1). The mandate applies to equities, corporate and municipal bonds, and exchange-traded funds listed on U.S. national securities exchanges, among other securities. Exempted instruments include government securities, which already settled at T+1, and certain mutual fund transactions that continue to settle at T+3 per the Investment Company Act.

The SEC's rationale for the accelerated cycle centred on reducing systemic counterparty risk, limiting margin calls during periods of market volatility (drawing on lessons from the January 2021 meme-stock events), and aligning U.S. markets with evolving global settlement norms. Canada and Mexico adopted coordinated T+1 mandates effective the same date, creating a trilateral North American T+1 standard that facilitates cross-border equity transactions between these markets while placing new operational strain on participants with exposure to markets running on different settlement conventions.

Regulatory Amendments and Affiliated Rules

Alongside the T+1 settlement rule, the SEC adopted amendments to:

  • Rule 15c6-2, requiring same-day affirmation of allocations and confirmations between broker-dealers and investment advisers
  • Rule 204 of Regulation SHO, adjusting close-out requirements for fails to deliver to align with the shorter settlement window
  • DTCC's DTC Settlement Service rules, updated to reflect shortened processing windows for clearance and settlement

Impact on Securities Lending Operations

Recall Timelines

T+1 settlement compresses the operational window available to beneficial owners and lending agents to recall securities required for corporate actions, proxy voting, or sale settlement. Under T+2, a lender selling a security had two business days to recall and receive the security back from the borrower. Under T+1, this window is effectively eliminated for same-day trade decisions. Securities lending programs must now implement real-time or near-real-time trade capture and recall initiation processes, with automated recall triggers integrated with the lending agent's order management and settlement systems. Programs relying on end-of-day recall batches will routinely generate settlement fails under T+1.

FX Settlement Mismatches for Foreign Participants

Non-US asset managers and institutional investors face a structural FX settlement mismatch under T+1. A foreign investor selling a US equity receives USD proceeds on T+1 but cannot access the corresponding FX conversion until the FX market settles at T+2 (for standard spot FX). This creates a one-day funding gap that must be bridged through:

  • Pre-funding arrangements with custodians or prime brokers willing to advance USD against anticipated FX settlement
  • Increased use of T+1 FX products, including non-deliverable forwards and same-day FX settlement mechanisms
  • Adjustment of investment mandates and cash management policies to accommodate the new timing mismatch

Canadian institutional investors accessing U.S. markets through CIBC Mellon's global custody and securities lending infrastructure are particularly affected, given the frequency of USD/CAD cross-currency settlement pairs.

Collateral Substitution Windows

Securities lending programs utilising non-cash collateral face tighter collateral substitution windows under T+1. When a borrower needs to substitute pledged collateral — for example, to meet its own settlement obligations — the substitution must clear within a single business day. This requires:

  • Same-day acceptance or rejection of substitution requests by lending agents
  • Intraday collateral eligibility screening against approved collateral schedules
  • Automated mark-to-market and margin call workflows to prevent under-collateralisation during the substitution window

DTCC and Industry Solutions

The Depository Trust & Clearing Corporation (DTCC) has introduced several operational enhancements to support T+1, including expanded CNS (Continuous Net Settlement) processing windows and enhanced same-day affirmation capabilities through its CTM (Central Trade Manager) platform. Industry bodies including SIFMA, ICI, and the Securities Finance Forum have published T+1 implementation guides covering securities lending-specific operational adjustments, which lending agents and beneficial owners should review alongside their own operational readiness assessments.
