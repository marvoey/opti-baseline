IFRS and Canadian GAAP: Key Differences for Alternative Funds

Canadian investment funds and their sponsors must choose between two accounting frameworks: International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) as adopted in Canada, and Canadian Generally Accepted Accounting Principles for private entities and not-for-profit organisations under Part II and Part III of the CPA Canada Handbook. For most reporting issuers, IFRS is mandatory. However, investment companies that qualify as "private enterprises" and are not reporting issuers may elect to apply Canadian GAAP under Part II of the CPA Canada Handbook, including the Accounting Guideline AcG-18 for investment companies. The choice of accounting framework has material implications for consolidation treatment, fair value measurement, and disclosure — all of which are particularly consequential for alternative investment funds with significant holdings in limited partnerships, real assets, and private equity.

IFRS 10 Consolidation vs. AcG-18

Under IFRS 10 Consolidated Financial Statements, an entity must consolidate all subsidiaries over which it has control, defined as power over the investee, exposure to variable returns, and the ability to use power to affect those returns. IFRS 10 includes an investment entity exception: entities meeting the definition of an "investment entity" are required to measure subsidiaries at fair value through profit or loss rather than consolidating them. The investment entity assessment under IFRS 10 requires careful analysis and must be made at the fund level, not the manager level.

AcG-18 under Canadian GAAP similarly provides that investment companies should measure their investments at fair value, without consolidation of investees, unless an investee is itself an operating entity that is not an investment company. The practical differences between IFRS 10 and AcG-18 in treatment of investee consolidation are relatively limited for pure-play investment funds, but diverge meaningfully for funds with hybrid structures or for fund-of-fund platforms.

Fair Value Measurement: IFRS 13 vs. Canadian GAAP

IFRS 13 Fair Value Measurement provides a unified, comprehensive definition of fair value as the price that would be received to sell an asset or paid to transfer a liability in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date (an exit price). IFRS 13 establishes a three-level fair value hierarchy:

  • Level 1: Quoted prices in active markets for identical assets or liabilities
  • Level 2: Inputs other than quoted prices that are observable, either directly or indirectly
  • Level 3: Unobservable inputs, requiring significant management judgment

Canadian GAAP under Part II does not include a directly equivalent standard; instead, fair value guidance is embedded within individual financial instrument standards (Sections 3856 and 3862). For alternative fund administrators, the practical effect is that IFRS 13's extensive disclosure requirements — including sensitivity analysis for Level 3 measurements and reconciliation of opening to closing balances — are not required under Canadian GAAP, reducing but also potentially obscuring the transparency of private asset valuations.

Reporting and Disclosure Implications

IAS 28 Equity Method vs. Canadian GAAP

Under IFRS, IAS 28 Investments in Associates and Joint Ventures requires the equity method of accounting for investments where an entity has significant influence (typically a 20%–50% ownership interest), unless the investment entity exception under IFRS 10 applies. When the investment entity exception applies, such investments are measured at fair value. Canadian GAAP under Section 3051 similarly requires the equity method for significant influence investments for operating companies, but investment companies applying AcG-18 measure all investments at fair value regardless of ownership percentage. This difference is material for alternative fund structures that hold minority stakes in real asset operating entities (e.g., infrastructure, real estate operating companies) where IFRS requires careful determination of whether the fund qualifies as an investment entity.

Limited Partnerships and Real Assets

Alternative investment funds frequently invest through limited partnership structures. Under both IFRS and Canadian GAAP, a general partner's interest in a limited partnership must be evaluated for control or significant influence. Where a fund holds LP interests representing a majority economic interest in an underlying partnership, IFRS 10 consolidation analysis (or the investment entity exception) must be applied. Canadian GAAP treatment is broadly similar, but the absence of IFRS 10's detailed guidance on structured entities (formerly IFRIC 12/SIC-12 / IFRS 10 Appendix B) can result in different conclusions for complex structures.

Disclosure differences for limited partnerships and real assets are significant: IFRS 12 Disclosure of Interests in Other Entities requires detailed disclosure of the nature, extent, and financial effects of interests in subsidiaries, associates, joint ventures, and unconsolidated structured entities. There is no equivalent Canadian GAAP standard with the same breadth of disclosure, which has implications for fund administrators preparing IFRS financial statements for institutional investors who have come to expect IFRS 12-level transparency.
