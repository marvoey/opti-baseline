Privacy and Data Governance in Canadian Recordkeeping

Canadian pension fund recordkeeping operations are subject to a multi-layered privacy and data governance framework that encompasses federal legislation, provincial private sector privacy laws, OSFI prudential expectations, and the pension-specific obligations arising under the Pension Benefits Standards Act, 1985 (PBSA). Pension administrators must ensure that personal information of plan members — including contribution histories, benefit entitlements, beneficiary designations, and contact details — is collected, used, disclosed, and retained in accordance with applicable privacy law, regardless of whether recordkeeping functions are performed in-house or delegated to a third-party administrator.

The foundational federal privacy statute for private-sector organisations — including federally regulated pension plan administrators — is the Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA). PIPEDA applies to personal information collected, used, or disclosed in the course of commercial activities, and requires organisations to obtain meaningful consent, limit collection to what is necessary for identified purposes, and protect information using appropriate security safeguards. PIPEDA is currently under reform through Bill C-27 (the Digital Charter Implementation Act, 2022), which, once enacted, will replace PIPEDA with the Consumer Privacy Protection Act (CPPA) and introduce significantly strengthened consent requirements, data portability rights, and a new Artificial Intelligence and Data Act.

Quebec Law 25 (Act 25)

Quebec's Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector (Law 25 / Bill 64) was amended in three phases, with the most significant requirements taking effect September 22, 2023. Pension administrators with plan members resident in Quebec must comply with materially more stringent obligations than PIPEDA currently requires, including:

  • Appointment of a named Privacy Officer responsible for compliance and published on the organisation's website
  • Mandatory Privacy Impact Assessments (PIAs) before any new collection or technology system involving personal information
  • Mandatory data minimisation — collection must be limited to what is strictly necessary for the specific, explicit purpose
  • Explicit consent required for any communication of personal information outside Quebec, including to other Canadian provinces or to foreign service providers
  • Breach notification to the Commission d'accès à l'information (CAI) within 72 hours of becoming aware of a "confidentiality incident" involving a serious injury risk

Alberta PIPA

Alberta's Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA) is Canada's other provincially enacted private-sector privacy statute, applying to provincial organisations operating in Alberta. Pension administrators with plan members in Alberta — and whose plan is regulated provincially rather than federally — must comply with Alberta PIPA, which is broadly analogous to PIPEDA but enforced by the Office of the Information and Privacy Commissioner of Alberta (OIPC Alberta).

Cross-Border Data Transfer and Residency Rules

Cross-Border Data Transfer Restrictions

Neither PIPEDA nor most provincial laws impose an outright prohibition on cross-border data transfers, but meaningful accountability requirements apply. Under PIPEDA and Quebec Law 25, organisations transferring personal information to third parties — including foreign service providers such as recordkeeping platforms hosted in the United States — must:

  • Conduct a PIA or equivalent risk assessment to identify and mitigate risks arising from foreign data access, including risk of access by foreign law enforcement under laws like the US Patriot Act or CLOUD Act
  • Implement contractual protections requiring the foreign service provider to maintain equivalent privacy standards
  • Notify affected individuals, where required by Quebec Law 25, that their personal information will be communicated outside Quebec and obtain their consent where required

OSFI Data Residency Expectations

OSFI's Guideline E-21, Operational Risk and Resilience, and its Technology and Cyber Risk Management Guideline (B-10, effective January 1, 2024) address data residency as part of the broader technology risk management framework. OSFI expects FRFIs to assess the legal and operational risks associated with storing or processing data in foreign jurisdictions and to maintain the ability to access data needed to continue critical operations regardless of any disruption affecting a foreign service provider or jurisdiction. Where pension plan administration data is hosted or processed outside Canada, a documented risk assessment and contractual safeguards satisfactory to OSFI are required.

Pension Administrator Responsibilities Under the PBSA

Section 7 of the PBSA requires pension plan administrators to maintain records sufficient to determine each member's entitlement to benefits. Records must be retained for a period specified in the regulations — generally, for the life of the plan and for a minimum of six years after the plan's termination. Where recordkeeping is outsourced, the plan administrator remains the accountable party and must maintain adequate oversight of the service provider, including periodic audits and access to data in the event of provider failure.

Incident Reporting Obligations

In the event of a privacy breach, Canadian pension administrators face multiple, potentially overlapping reporting obligations:

  • PIPEDA: Report breaches of security safeguards involving a real risk of significant harm to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada (OPC) as soon as feasible, and notify affected individuals at the same time
  • Quebec Law 25: Notify the CAI within 72 hours; notify affected individuals without delay where a serious injury risk exists
  • OSFI: Notify OSFI of technology incidents and cyber incidents in accordance with OSFI's technology and cyber incident reporting requirements, with initial notification expected within 24 hours for material incidents
  • PBSA: OSFI may require reporting of incidents affecting the integrity of plan member records as part of plan administrator supervisory obligations
