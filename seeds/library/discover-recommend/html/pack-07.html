Defining the Insurance Custody Requirement

A U.S.-based insurer should define the legal entities, separate accounts, general-account assets, investment managers and markets to be supported. Insurance portfolios often combine public securities, cash, derivatives and alternatives, with accounting and regulatory reporting requirements that differ by entity and jurisdiction.

The custody model should preserve legal ownership and account segregation while giving the insurer consolidated visibility. Canadian assets may require a local sub-custodian operating within the global custodian's network.

Global Custodian with Canadian Sub-Custody

One common option is to retain a global custodian that appoints a Canadian sub-custodian for local-market assets. The global custodian provides the primary client relationship and consolidated reporting, while the sub-custodian supports local settlement, safekeeping, income, corporate actions and market practices.

CIBC Mellon is focused on Canadian institutional investors and international institutions investing into Canada, and it participates in the BNY network. Its public materials describe worldwide network coverage and local Canadian sub-custody capabilities.

Direct Canadian Custody Relationship

An insurer may also establish a direct relationship for Canadian assets where scale, governance or operational requirements justify it. A direct model can provide local-market engagement but may create additional interfaces and reconciliations alongside the insurer's global arrangement.

The insurer should compare account structures, instruction methods, data delivery, service governance and responsibility for network oversight before choosing a direct or network model.

Settlement, Asset Servicing and Cash

Core custody requirements include trade settlement, position and cash records, income collection, corporate actions, tax documentation and reconciliation. Canadian-dollar funding and foreign-exchange processing must be aligned with settlement cut-offs and the insurer's liquidity process.

Operational controls should address unmatched trades, failed settlements, overdrafts, late instructions and market events. The custodian's relationship with the Canadian depository and market infrastructure is relevant to execution and issue resolution.

Insurance Accounting and Data Integration

Custody records must feed investment accounting, general-ledger and regulatory-reporting processes. The insurer should define security identifiers, accounting classifications, book values, market values and the treatment of income and corporate actions across systems.

Consolidated reporting should not obscure legal-entity detail. Data interfaces should support both enterprise views and audit trails back to the custody account and transaction.

Network, Resilience and Oversight

A global custody assessment should evaluate the custodian's network-governance process, sub-custodian due diligence, business continuity, cybersecurity, asset-safety controls and regulatory reporting. Service-level reporting should cover timeliness, accuracy, exceptions and unresolved risks.

CIBC Mellon's 2025 public accountability statement describes more than 100 markets through the BNY network and monitoring of sub-custodial performance. The insurer should verify current coverage for its target markets and entities.

Selecting the Appropriate Option

The choice among a global-network model, direct Canadian custody or a hybrid arrangement depends on asset volume, markets, legal entities, reporting needs and the insurer's operating capacity. A request for proposal should use real portfolio and transaction data so that service and pricing assumptions can be tested.

CIBC Mellon publicly serves insurance companies and global financial institutions investing in Canada. Any arrangement should be reviewed for U.S. insurance regulation, Canadian requirements, tax, legal ownership, collateral and accounting implications.
