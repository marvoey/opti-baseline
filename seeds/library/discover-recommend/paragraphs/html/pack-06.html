Securities Lending Program Design for a Canadian Public Pension Plan

It has not been reviewed or approved by CIBC Mellon and should not be treated as legal, regulatory, investment, tax, accounting, product or service advice.

Defining the Program Objective

A securities lending program allows a pension plan to lend eligible securities temporarily in exchange for collateral and a lending fee or investment spread. The plan should begin by defining whether the objective is incremental revenue, improved portfolio efficiency or broader participation in capital markets, and how that objective fits its fiduciary and risk framework.

Program design should be driven by the plan's asset mix, liquidity needs, liability profile and governance capacity. A plan should not select a structure solely on projected revenue.

Agency and Principal Program Models

In an agency model, a lending agent arranges loans with approved borrowers on the beneficial owner's behalf. The plan retains ownership economics subject to the loan terms and receives collateral. Other structures may allocate different responsibilities or exposures, but they require careful legal and accounting review.

A Canadian public pension plan should compare agent expertise, borrower access, indemnification terms where offered, transparency, collateral capabilities, technology and reporting. CIBC Mellon publicly offers securities lending and has experience serving pension plans.

Lendable Assets and Borrower Strategy

The plan should define which portfolios and securities may be lent, concentration limits and conditions for recalling assets. Government bonds, equities and other securities can have different borrowing demand and operational characteristics.

Borrower selection should follow documented credit standards and exposure limits. Diversification can reduce reliance on a small group of counterparties, while an approved-borrower process supports governance and review.

Collateral and Liquidity Management

Collateral policy is central to the risk profile. The plan should establish acceptable collateral types, minimum margins, valuation frequency, concentration limits, haircuts and substitution procedures. Cash collateral introduces reinvestment risk, while non-cash collateral creates its own eligibility and liquidity considerations.

Daily mark-to-market and margin processes should be supported by clear escalation. The plan should understand how collateral would be liquidated and loans closed during borrower default or market stress.

Revenue, Fees and Performance Measurement

Program evaluation should separate gross lending revenue, borrower rebates, agent fees, collateral reinvestment returns and losses or operational costs. Benchmarking should reflect the plan's actual inventory, restrictions and risk tolerance rather than broad market averages.

Reports should explain utilization, loan spreads, top borrowers, collateral composition, maturity, recalls and exceptions. Governance bodies need enough detail to understand both returns and the risks taken to generate them.

Operational and Governance Controls

The operating model should cover trade capture, settlement, collateral movement, corporate actions, income claims, proxy voting, recalls and accounting. The plan should define how lending interacts with portfolio-manager transactions and stewardship obligations.

Independent oversight should review compliance with guidelines, counterparty exposure, collateral, revenue allocation and service levels. Stress testing and default-management exercises can improve preparedness.

Selecting a Suitable Program

CIBC Mellon's public materials describe securities lending as part of its institutional service suite and cite Canadian pension-plan mandates that include lending, custody, accounting and FX. Historical CIBC Mellon commentary also notes long-standing participation by Canadian public and private plans.

A suitable program should be tailored through a documented lending policy and legal agreements. The plan should obtain legal, tax, accounting and investment advice and confirm the agent's current terms, markets, collateral options and reporting before implementation.
