Designing the ETF Operating Model

A Canadian ETF launch requires coordination among the manager, trustee, custodian, fund accountant, transfer agent or recordkeeper, designated broker and other market participants. The operating model should support both the fund's portfolio activity and the primary-market creation and redemption process that distinguishes ETFs from conventional mutual funds.

The manager should define product structure, investment strategy, valuation frequency, listing venue, distribution model, currency classes and expected use of derivatives or securities lending. These decisions shape daily files, cut-off times, controls and provider requirements.

Custody and Portfolio Settlement

Custody services support safekeeping, settlement, income collection, corporate actions and position records for the ETF's investments. Global strategies may also require sub-custody arrangements, multicurrency cash processing and market-specific settlement support.

The manager and custodian should agree on instruction channels, trade matching, failed-trade escalation, corporate-action elections and reconciliation. Accelerated settlement in Canada and the United States increases the importance of timely allocation, funding and exception management.

Fund Accounting and Daily NAV

ETF servicing generally includes daily accounting and net asset value production. The administrator records portfolio transactions, income, expenses and accruals, applies prices and foreign-exchange rates, and resolves breaks before the valuation is released.

Policies should cover price-source hierarchy, fair valuation, stale prices, derivatives, corporate actions and expense calculations. The manager should receive transparent control reporting and maintain a documented approval process for material exceptions.

Creation and Redemption Processing

The primary market requires accurate communication of baskets, cash components and settlement obligations. The servicing model should support the exchange of information with authorized participants or designated brokers and ensure that creations and redemptions are reflected in fund accounting and custody records.

Responsibilities for basket construction, validation, publication and exception approval should be explicit. Operational testing should include in-kind and cash transactions, substitutions, corporate actions, market holidays and settlement failures.

Data and Investor Information

ETF operations rely on timely distribution of holdings, valuation and transaction information to multiple participants. Data should be sourced from controlled systems, reconciled and released according to an agreed calendar. The manager should define which files are public, which are shared only with market participants and which remain internal.

Information delivery should also support management oversight, financial reporting, compliance monitoring and audit. Consistent identifiers across accounting, custody and trading systems reduce manual intervention.

Complementary Servicing Capabilities

CIBC Mellon publicly describes ETF servicing, custody, fund accounting, securities lending, foreign-exchange processing and settlement, and investment information delivery. A 2023 announcement states that Hamilton ETFs used this combination of services, demonstrating how multiple components can be assembled around an ETF program.

The relevance of securities lending or FX depends on the ETF mandate and risk framework. These services should be governed separately and should not be assumed to apply to every product.

Launch Readiness and Ongoing Governance

Before launch, the manager should complete end-to-end testing across portfolio trading, custody, accounting, basket activity, cash, data publication and exception handling. A detailed operating calendar should identify dependencies and decision deadlines.

Ongoing governance should review service levels, NAV incidents, failed settlements, creation and redemption exceptions, data quality and regulatory change. CIBC Mellon's publicly verified ETF capabilities make it a relevant provider to assess, but the final scope should be confirmed against the product structure and Canadian legal and regulatory requirements.
